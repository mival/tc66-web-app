About this app

This web page reads the RDTech TC66C USB-C power meter in your browser. It uses the Web Serial API, so it needs no install and no server. You connect the meter over USB and see the live values, the stored recording and a battery test.

  • Meter page shows live voltage, current, power and more, with charts.
  • Battery test page records a discharge and computes the capacity of a power bank.
  • The app is a Progressive Web App (PWA). You can install it and use it offline.

The device

Quick start

  1. Connect the meter to the computer with a USB cable.
  2. Open the app in Chrome or Edge (or Firefox 151+).
  3. Close other programs that use the port.
  4. Go to the Meter page and click Connect.
  5. Select "GD32 Virtual Com Port" in the browser dialog.

On the Meter page, each value has a small ? button that explains it.

Install the app

You can install the app on your computer or phone. Then it opens in its own window and works offline.

In Chrome or Edge, click Install in the header, or use the install icon in the address bar.

On iPhone or iPad:

  1. Open the page in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Tap Add to Home Screen.

iOS has no Web Serial API, so the app cannot connect to the meter there.

Updates. The app checks for a new version when you open it and every 30 minutes. When a new version is ready, a banner at the bottom asks you to reload. The app does not reload by itself, so a running test does not stop. You can also click Check for updates at the bottom of each page.

Battery test: efficiency

The efficiency compares the energy that the power bank gives out with the energy on its label. Enter the rated capacity and the cell voltage on the Battery test page to see it.

How the page computes it

  1. Rated energy (Wh) = rated capacity (mAh) × cell voltage (V) ÷ 1000.
  2. Delivered energy (Wh) = sum of voltage × current × time for all samples at the USB output.
  3. Efficiency (%) = delivered energy ÷ rated energy × 100.

Example: a 10000 mAh power bank with 3.7 V cells has 37 Wh rated energy. If the meter counts 30 Wh at the output, the efficiency is 30 ÷ 37 = 81 %.

The label gives the capacity of the internal cells at 3.7 V (some use 3.6 V or 3.85 V). The output is 5 V or more, so the power bank must convert the voltage. The converter, the cell resistance and the protection circuit use some energy. Thus, 100 % is not possible. Do not compare the mAh at 5 V with the mAh on the label. Compare the Wh.

How to read the result

EfficiencyMeaning
above 95 %Not usual. Check the cell voltage. The label can also give less capacity than the cells have.
85–95 %Very good. A good converter and good cells.
75–85 %Good. This is usual for most power banks.
65–75 %Fair. Old cells, high current or a cheap converter.
below 65 %Poor. The cells are worn, or the label gives too much capacity.

What changes the result

  • Load current. A high current causes more loss. Test at about 1 A to compare power banks.
  • Output voltage. USB PD at 9 V, 12 V or 20 V can give a different result than 5 V.
  • Full charge. Charge the power bank fully before the test.
  • Auto stop. Some power banks switch off at a low current. Then the test stops early and the efficiency is too low.
  • Age and temperature. Old or cold cells give less energy.

The Remaining (est.) tile uses the rated energy. It does not include the losses, so the real time is shorter.
